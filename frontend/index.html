How it works

Market making and treasury diversification become the same trade. The treasury sells only into real buy demand, at its own pace. No OTC dumps, no custody handover.

Post a mandate

The treasury deposits token and stablecoin into the hook and sets the rules: target mix, minimum depth, fee cap, epochs.

Managers bid a bond

Market makers compete by posting a first-loss bond. The highest bond wins the mandate; outbid bonds are claimable straight away.

Steer, don't hold

The hook owns the liquidity. The manager moves ranges and sets fees inside the caps. Funds never leave the hook.

Every swap is checked

In range, deep enough, on pace, measured on a TWAP so one swap can't fake it. A clean epoch pays the manager; a breach slashes the bond to the treasury.

The leash

Withdrawing is the easy attack to block. The real one is mispricing: moving the price, then trading against the treasury's own liquidity. Leash prices every move against a time-weighted average.

The manager can

  • Move liquidity between ranges, as often as the market needs.
  • Set the swap fee, up to the treasury's cap.
  • Earn a cut of swap fees for every clean epoch, plus an onchain track record.

The leash stops

  • Withdrawals. No function sends inventory to the manager.
  • Off-market ranges. Liquidity must sit near the TWAP, not a price pushed for one block.
  • Fee gouging. The fee can never exceed the mandate's cap.
  • Outside LPs. The pool is the treasury's book alone, so depth and mix are measured exactly.

A market maker you never have to trust with the keys. Free to make markets. Not free to run.
